Grad-CAM Mathematical Formulation

METHODOLOGY
Step 1

Gradient Backpropagation

Compute the gradient of the predicted class score \(y^c\) with respect to feature map activations \(A^k\) of the final convolutional layer:

\[\frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{ij}^k}\]
Step 2

Channel Importance Weights

Calculate neuron importance weights \(\alpha_k^c\) by performing global average pooling over spatial width \(u\) and height \(v\):

\[\alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z} \sum_i \sum_j \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{ij}^k}\]
Step 3

ReLU Linear Combination

Perform a weighted sum of forward feature maps and apply a rectified linear unit to preserve features positively contributing to class \(c\):

\[L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\left(\sum_k \alpha_k^c A^k\right)\]

What Grad-CAM Demonstrates

  • Identifies regions where texture, boundary contrast, and pixel gradients heavily weighted the softmax output.
  • Detects whether the model is focusing on legitimate brain tissue rather than background noise, watermarks, or skull edges.
  • Enables clinicians and researchers to verify that model attention correlates with suspected pathology.

What Grad-CAM Does NOT Mean

  • It does not produce surgical tumor margins or volumetric segmentations.
  • Warm red areas are not guaranteed tumor tissue; they represent model sensitivity.
  • It cannot substitute for histologic, biopsy, or radiologist-certified verification.
